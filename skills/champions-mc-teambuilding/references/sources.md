# Team-building sources

Read and synthesized on **2026-10-02**. The instructions are original condensed
guidance, not copied chapters. VGC Guide is one editorial source; its multiple
authors do not make its chapters independent metagame datasets.

## Expert methodology, historical examples

- **[Intent](https://www.vgcguide.com/intent)** — Aaron Traylor. No date displayed.
  Give the central idea and important choices a reason; compare intended jobs
  with what happens in practice. This informs the skill's role explanations.
- **[How to Pull it all Together](https://www.vgcguide.com/how-to-pull-it-all-together)**
  — Aaron Traylor and Wolfe Glick. No date displayed. Build around an interaction,
  add compatible partners, examine groups of four, then refine sets through play.
  This informs the flexible core-to-complete-team workflow.
- **[Cores and Modes](https://www.vgcguide.com/cores-and-modes)** — Wolfe Glick.
  No date displayed. Distinguish the team-defining core from optional plans;
  examine whether an added pair constrains selection. This informs the mode audit.
- **[How Do You Know if a Team You've Made is Good?](https://www.vgcguide.com/how-do-you-know-if-a-team-youve-made-is-good)**
  — Aaron Traylor. No date displayed. Evaluate proactive options, matchup
  severity, and how the team functions after opponents learn its sets.
  This informs the testing and iteration criteria.

These chapters contain examples from older games and regulations. Their Pokemon,
items, Dynamax interactions, and traditional EV spreads are not M-C defaults.
The site's [Preface](https://www.vgcguide.com/preface), by Aaron Traylor, explicitly
distinguishes its long-lived principles from current metagame coverage.
