# Create and convert a winning position

Use this with the actual selected four and open sheet, before preview and live
play. It supplies attacking objectives, not an extra live-turn checklist. Keep
the working plan to **next obstacle -> forcing pair -> next-board payoff** and
submit promptly under the [BO3 cycle](headless-battle-cycle.md). Numerical
damage, survival and Speed claims still require actual MCP results; unknown
investments and unsupported states remain explicit.

## Choose the route, then create pressure

Select four as a connected plan, not four favorable individual matchups:

- **Front two:** Against a plausible opposing lead, name their shared first-turn
  task: pressure the obstacle, deny an enabling action, establish useful control,
  or force a defense that creates position. Give a materially different opposing
  opening and how the pair changes its response; neither lead is revealed intent.
- **Back two:** Give each reserve its job, entry condition and first useful
  action window. A checked pivot, a threatened foe's defense or a purposeful
  support trade can create that window; none grants a switch-turn attack or
  assumes the other foe cooperates. Reserves enter when their jobs are needed.
- **Selection cost:** Compare one meaningful alternative four. Name the important
  role or answer left out and why this four's route is still preferable. When
  fast and Trick Room modes coexist, assign the opening mode, beneficiary and
  transition condition; do not set Room or expose a fast reserve by habit.

Before crediting a fast opening against an available opposing control setter,
name its first useful attack if that control resolves: the actual beneficiary,
cover or contained expiration window. Protecting the fast user or replacing it
with another fast reserve does not itself answer the setter. Compare pressure,
eligible denial or reversal with accepting control and its concrete conversion;
reversal still needs delivery and can be reversed again. Keeping pressure or
trading through control can be best when it earns the remaining route. Neither
the setter's selection nor its action is certain, and no fixed lead is required.

Keep the compact route as **opposing opening -> front-two forcing task ->
back-two jobs/entry windows -> next pair and finish -> condition changing it**.
The selected four must contain a credible closing attacker or pair and a route through its obstacle;
the bench supplies neither an answer nor an entry in this game.

**Compare the second-turn position before taking convenient damage.** Trigger
this when chip leaves the blocking plan intact, a needed actor/reset may be spent,
a reserve can gain a useful entry, or a speed/field window changes. Compare the
immediate attack pair with one available attack-plus-positioning line: our pair -> plausible
opposing pair -> remaining front/back and control -> next useful pair. Include
their attacking/punishing branch as well as the defense we hope to force. Name
what the setup, pivot or Protect buys; surviving or switching alone is no payoff.
Keep this within the current bounded choice, not an extra research/model round.
Use the existing actual MCP checks for material delivery or removal dependencies.

Removal pressure, useful spread damage or denying control can force a defense
and earn the partner's setup or reserve entry. Immediate attacks remain best when
they remove the obstacle, deliver a worthwhile trade or finish before a positional
line pays off. Do not switch merely to follow a preview script or protect an
unneeded resource while giving away setup. Revise the route after the actual reply.

**Recheck repeated pressure before spending another support turn.** When the
same surviving foe keeps damaging or denying our required actors, identify what
the next pair changes about its next attack: delivered disruption, reduced
pressure, removal, or a useful entry that can answer the surviving board.
If Protect already erased our pair or a drain restored the target while removing
a contributor, start from that failed conversion before ranking another hit.
Changing one target, adding support or naming a reserve is not the lasting change;
identify the useful progress through the compatible defense-plus-partner reply.
Damage, a stat drop or healing denial into its partner alone establishes no such
change. Resolve the required actor's delivery and any removal deadline with the
existing MCP checks; do not assume the dangerous hit becomes harmless. If the
pair leaves that pressure intact, compare an attack or disruption into its source,
protection of the required actor, or an explicit necessary trade before repeating
it. Support removal remains useful when it actually opens that route or its
partner cannot exploit the turn; do not make targeting the attacker compulsory.

**Exploit the defensive response.** If a threatened target is likely to Protect
or switch, ask what that response lets our other action accomplish. Attack its
partner, set useful control, position the closing attacker, or hit a recipient
that cannot absorb both attacks comfortably. Double the target when denying its
action or removing the obstacle justifies both actions and its partner's likely
progress. A sole remaining foe has no active partner to exploit a protected
double; that is a different cost from ignoring an active setup threat. Keep the
attack/sacrifice branch as well as the defensive read.

Retain the route's dependency in the compact plan: finisher -> resource needed
until its window -> obstacle to remove -> useful entry or attack. Revisit that
dependency before sacrificing a weather/control resource or choosing support
removal over the attacker currently denying the route. A support action that
already resolved is not cancelled by knocking its user out afterward. Preserve
a needed reset while the opposing overwrite remains available; allow a purposeful
trade when a checked remaining route no longer needs that reset. These are
priorities within the existing paired plan, not a separate live checklist.

Refresh the obstacle after observed offensive drops, item loss or a changed
field. Rank what each foe can now deliver into our actual remaining pair and
resources, rather than retaining its earlier threat label. A weakened attacker
may fall below its still-dangerous partner; compare partner removal or disruption
before spending another attack finishing the weakened slot. Reduced offense is
not harmlessness: it may still finish a damaged ally, support its partner, or
reset by switching. Check material damage/order through MCP and retain that
counterbranch; no numerical safety or winning continuation follows from a drop alone.

## Spend temporary advantages

Before a weather-dependent immediate attack, include an available opposing
weather entry in the opening pair. Weather loss can introduce a charging turn
and commit a Choice holder to a later release, rather than merely changing
damage. Name the useful charge/release/reset continuation or choose pressure
that still works after the overwrite. Available weather change is a leading
branch, not certainty; a covered charge or an unavailable opposing setter can
still justify the attack. Use current weather again after every overwrite or
expiration, including the actual type of Weather Ball.

After gaining Room, Tailwind, favorable weather, a damaging boost, or a useful
Fake Out window, identify the useful attack or entry it buys. Repeated passive
turns can spend that advantage without bringing the endgame closer. Protect can
still be the strongest pressure play when the partner removes an obstacle,
forces their defense, or positions the next attacker; two actions that merely
delay need a reason, such as expiring opposing control or unavoidable containment.

Plan across the last useful control turn. Remove or weaken the obstacle while
the advantage applies and arrange a surviving pair for expiration. Do not reverse
Room or use a Speed drop merely because the move is available: name who benefits
now and after expiration. A move chosen for chip can have an adverse Speed
consequence.

Before setting or renewing Room, identify the next required attacker, including
a fast reserve likely to enter after the current trade. Compare that attack's
eligible order with and without Room using actual MCP Speed when material;
naming a slow current ally alone does not establish a useful continuation. Check
what that first attack accomplishes against both foes and their defense, rather
than treating favorable order as a finish. Contrast a slow attacker delivering
needed progress on the last control turn or a purposeful sacrifice that earns
its entry: retain those useful control lines instead of banning Room beside a
fast reserve.

In a retained clean game, Psychic removed one foe while its partner Protected;
later Hyper Voice removed the replacement through another Protect. Sylveon then
removed Salamence while Farigiraf switched Golisopod into the closing position
before Room expired. That observed sequence illustrates conversion and useful
partner progress. It does not prove the earlier Politoed trade was optimal,
establish an unseen investment, or guarantee the same removals on another board.

### Break screens and recovery-backed setup

Read the opposing engine together: mitigation buys setup turns, support protects
the attacker, and draining recovery can erase scattered chip while dealing damage.
Choose a removal objective and the paired actions that reach it, then name the
follow-up that closes the game. Commit enough pressure to finish that objective;
reassess after Protect, switches or new threats instead of attacking the same
slot by habit. Do not spread recoverable damage merely because both hits connect.

- **Remove the setter/support** when denying further screens, disruption or
  positioning opens our attacker's route. Check what the opposing attacker gains
  during that removal and our answer to its resulting boosted board. Removing
  the setter does not clear screens already established; name the attack, screen
  removal or contained expiration route that still delivers the finish.
- **Remove or disable the attacker** when it is the immediate win condition and
  its support cannot prevent a feasible removal. Check screens, Protect,
  redirection, switches and both opposing actions before committing a double.
  Name what our partner achieves if the target defends, and what remains after
  the attacker is removed. A support KO is not compulsory when it leaves the
  actual sweeper free to win.

Use available disruption to create a conversion window. Healing prevention can
stop a recovery loop; Psychic Noise's Heal Block can also make a draining attack
unusable, as described in the [mechanics check](mc-rules-and-tools.md#psychic-noise-can-deny-a-draining-attack).
Check the actual recipient, application and remaining lifetime against Protect,
immunity, ability, move order and any switch. A fresh application can buy the
partner's next attack or entry after the provider leaves, even when the foe has
already acted this turn; it cannot prevent an already executed drain. An active
Noise rehit does not renew its block. Use the mechanics check to distinguish
that later conversion from same-turn prevention.
Name the partner's useful attack or entry and the next-turn removal before the
denial ends. Denial alone is not a finish, and non-draining coverage, further
boosting or the other opposing slot can still threaten us. Preserve a needed
priority blocker or other support only while it enables that conversion.

Stalling screens is useful when setup, recovery and opposing partner progress
are contained and the surviving pair can exploit expiration. Compare the board
after those defensive turns, including screen renewal and our attacker's entry.
Repeated Protects or pivots that give free boosts or a vulnerable drain target
can strengthen their endgame despite spending screen turns. Prefer active
removal or disruption there; prefer preservation when it actually earns a safer
finish. Use only the actual team's tools, and leave unsupported screened damage
or unknown Speed benchmarks unverified rather than inventing numerical certainty.

### Hand off from Room to a fast cleaner

A mixed-speed four can spend Room on its slow attackers while reserving a fast
cleaner for expiration. Name the slow attacker to preserve, the obstacle it must
remove or chip, and the partner that closes beside the cleaner. On the last useful
Room turn, compare continued pressure, protecting that attacker, a covered switch,
or a spent support's trade that earns the cleaner's entry. Retain the setter,
weather setter or other answer when the remaining route still needs it; otherwise
its useful job may be creating that entry. A switch-in cannot attack that turn,
and a post-KO entry still needs both opposing actions checked on the next board.

Check the first turn after expiration, opposing Room reversal, priority and any
needed weather. Do not reset Room after losing its slow attacker unless a remaining
beneficiary can survive and convert the new window. A cleaner holding a Choice item
needs a useful first attack into plausible switch recipients and a feasible reset
if its lock becomes exploitable. Earlier entry can still be necessary to remove an
obstacle, stop denial or avoid immediate loss; keeping the cleaner reserved is a
plan with contingencies, not a rule that overrides the current board.

## Trade for the finish, not just for survival

Compare trades by the remaining threats and actions, not by Pokemon count alone.
A spent support can be worth trading for a safe attacker entry or an otherwise
unavailable obstacle removal; preserving it at the cost of the sole damage answer
can lose the route. A sacrifice must buy a stated payoff and leave an answer to
the opponent's remaining mode. Check a feasible preservation line too: if Protect
plus the partner's attack earns the same progress, taking the hit may be needless.
An entry is not automatically safe just because it follows a KO; evaluate both
opposing actions and the new turn.

Keep pressure through conditional priority rather than treating it only as a
last-resort button. Sucker Punch can deny a pending attack or let its partner
finish a different target. If Protect, redirection or a switch is a leading
response, compare an ordinary attack, alternate target or positioning play that
uses that response. This preserves Sucker Punch's threatening role without
counting damage into a nonattacking or already-acted target. See the
[pending-attack mechanics](mc-rules-and-tools.md#sucker-punch-needs-a-pending-attack).

## Choose risk that improves the route

When ahead, convert the advantage with lines that cover their practical escape:
avoid an unnecessary hard read if ordinary paired pressure preserves the finish.
When behind, compare a plausible winning branch with the safer line's actual
future. A defensive move that leaves no feasible answer is not preferable merely
because it survives this turn. Take a supported read when it creates a route
that the alternatives lack; name the response being exploited and what happens
if wrong. Do not call a speculative branch guaranteed, invent a probability, or
make needless risky plays because the user asked for aggression.

Between games, adapt the forcing sequence to what the opponent revealed, and
consider their counter-adjustment. A repeated Protect can become an opportunity
to pressure the partner, but one use does not establish a habit. After every BO3,
review one created opportunity and one missed conversion when present, alongside
the required failures and positive evidence. Ask whether our actions moved toward
the stated finish, whether a passive turn spent control, and what the smallest
testable improvement is. Promote a reusable lesson with a counterexample; keep
match-specific error records and unsupported hypotheses private. At the batch
boundary, improve shared triggers/checks for repeated application failures under
the cycle's [shared-guidance checkpoint](headless-battle-cycle.md#update-shared-guidance-before-the-next-batch).
The [regression
cases](battle-regression-cases.md) exercise both aggression and restraint.
At each batch boundary, use the cycle's
[pattern review](headless-battle-cycle.md#synthesize-each-completed-batch) to
carry successful sequences and checked answers forward, then verify their
application at later eligible opportunities.
